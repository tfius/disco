On W×H toroidal Life grids, the maximum population of a still life is exactly floor(W·H/2), verified exhaustively for W ∈ {2..7} (via a row-pair transfer DP over cyclic row configurations, with H ∈ 3..16 for W ≤ 6 and H ∈ 3..9 for W = 7, plus brute force for H = 2 and cross-validation on 3×3, 2×4, 3×4, 4×4, 3×5). The bound is attained by stripe patterns: alternating full live rows (rows 0, 2, 4, …) form a still life whenever H is even, and full live columns whenever W is even (verified for all such sizes up to 16×16); when both W and H are odd the maximum drops only to (WH−1)/2, never lower. No still life ever exceeds population WH/2 — Elkies' infinite-plane density bound 1/2 is exact and tight on these toruses, by symmetry S(W,H) = S(H,W), so the law covers min(W,H) ≤ 7.
