On the 32×32 toroidal Life grid, the attractor ("ash") density of random soups is nearly independent of initial density p over a broad plateau, then collapses at a sharp high-p death cliff. Concretely, with soups `life.soup(seed, 32, 32, p)` run to their attractor (cycle detection, cap 4096 generations — every run below settles): (1) plateau — for p ∈ {0.35, 0.45, 0.55} with seeds 930000 + int(p·100)·100 + k, k = 0..19, the mean attractor density (mean cycle population / 1024) lies in [0.020, 0.040] for each p and the three means differ pairwise by less than 0.008, so Life largely forgets p there; (2) death cliff — with seeds 920000 + i·100 + k (k = 0..19, p = i/50): at p = 0.70 no run dies out (0/20 empty, mean attractor density > 0.015), the empty-attractor fraction first reaches ≥ 1/2 at p = 0.78 (p = 0.76 gives < 1/2), and at p = 0.86 all 20 runs end completely empty. Attractor periods throughout are almost all 1 or 2 (blinker/still-life ash); transients peak around p ≈ 0.45 (order several hundred generations) and drop to a handful of steps at both extremes.
