On W×H toroidal Life grids, the standard glider `{(0,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)}` behaves as a true spaceship iff min(W,H) ≥ 5: with transient 0 and constant population 5, its state at t+4 is exactly the t-state translated by (1,1), and its full return period is exactly 4·lcm(W,H) (so 5×5 gives 20, 7×16 gives 448). The naive "needs 2 empty columns of clearance" intuition is false — width 5 already suffices. Below the threshold the glider is destroyed by wrap self-interference and never recovers translation symmetry: on 4×H for H ≥ 6 it dies out completely (fixed point 0 after a ≤6-step transient), while special small cases hit nontrivial oscillators (4×4: a period-8, non-translation oscillator whose population cycles through 5,4,5,5,5,4,5,5; 3×H: dies or reaches small oscillators, e.g. period 14 on 3×16). In no tested sub-threshold case (W ≤ 4, H ≤ 16) is any cycle state a translate of the glider.
