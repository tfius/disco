On the 4×5 toroidal Life grid there exists a genuine localized traveling wave that breaks the infinite-grid speed limit: a population-8 pattern (representative state 7082, bit = r·4+c, e.g. rows `.#.#/.#.#/##.#/#.../....`) whose single Life step is an exact pure-row translation, f(s) = T(1,0)(s) (mirror forms translate by (4,0)), so it moves 1 cell per generation — speed c, twice the c/2 orthogonal ship limit of the infinite plane — returning to itself with period 5. Exhaustively over all 2^20 states, the 4×5 torus has exactly 8 drifting cycles that are "localized" (representative has both an empty row and an empty column); all are period 5, population 8, each satisfies f(s) = T(±1,0)(s), and their 40 cycle states form exactly one orbit under the torus symmetry group (translations × horizontal/vertical flips) — it is a single object up to symmetry. Both wraps are essential: the same cell set re-embedded on 4×6 or 5×5 is not a translate of itself after one step, so this is a wrap-powered wave with no infinite-grid counterpart. On the smaller widths nothing like it exists — 4×2, 4×3, 4×4 have zero localized drifting cycles (all their drifting cycles span every row or every column).
