Among the 256 elementary cellular automata rules, exactly six — {15, 51, 85, 170, 204, 240} — are bijective (permutations of state space) on periodic (cyclic) binary tapes for every width N tested from 4 through 13. These are precisely the rules whose next-cell value depends on at most one neighbor cell (pure shifts and/or complements): 204=identity, 51=complement, 170=shift-left, 15=shift-right(complement pattern), 85/240 = shift+complement combos. No other rule is bijective across that full N range — every other rule fails bijectivity (collision in global map) for at least one N in {4,...,13}.
